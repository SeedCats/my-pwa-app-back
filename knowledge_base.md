# Health & Wellness Knowledge Base for RAG

## 1. Body Mass Index (BMI)
**Source:** World Health Organization (WHO)

**WHO BMI Classifications for Adults:**
* **Underweight:** < 18.5
* **Normal weight:** 18.5 – 24.9
* **Overweight:** 25.0 – 29.9
* **Obesity (Class I):** 30.0 – 34.9
* **Obesity (Class II):** 35.0 – 39.9
* **Extreme Obesity (Class III):** $\ge$ 40.0

*Note:* BMI values are age-independent and the same for both sexes. However, BMI may not correspond to the same degree of fatness in different populations due to different body proportions.

## 2. Heart Rate
**Source:** American Heart Association (AHA) / WHO

**Definition:** Heart rate, or pulse, is the number of times your heart beats per minute (bpm). 

**Resting Heart Rate Guidelies:**
* **Normal Adult:** 60 to 100 beats per minute (bpm).
* **Athletes:** A well-trained athlete might have a normal resting heart rate closer to 40 beats a minute.

**Factors Affecting Heart Rate:**
* **Age, fitness, and activity levels.**
* **Emotions:** Stress, anxiety, or being "extremely happy or sad" can elevate your pulse.
* **Body size:** Usually doesn't change pulse, but if deeply obese, it might be higher.
* **Medications:** Beta blockers tend to slow the heart rate down.
* **Cardiovascular Disease:** Irregular or abnormally high/low rates (tachycardia > 100 bpm, bradycardia < 60 bpm) may indicate issues if symptomatic.

## 3. Stress
**Source:** World Health Organization (WHO)

**Definition:** Stress can be defined as a state of worry or mental tension caused by a difficult situation. Stress is a natural human response that prompts us to address challenges and threats in our lives. Everyone experiences stress to some degree.

**Physiological Response:**
* When stressed, the body releases hormones such as adrenaline and cortisol.
* **Immediate effects:** Increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, faster breathing, and muscle tension.
* **Long-term effects:** Chronic stress can contribute to severe health problems, including cardiovascular disease, high blood pressure, weakened immune system, and mental health disorders like depression and anxiety.

**Stress Management (WHO Recommendations):**
* Keep a daily routine.
* Get plenty of sleep (7-9 hours for adults).
* Connect with others and talk about feelings.
* Eat a healthy diet and exercise regularly.
* Limit time following stressful news.
